CONTENT_BRIEF:
Explain the tragedy of the commons as an economic mechanism, not a moral slogan. Use a shared pasture example: one herder adding one extra cow captures private benefit, while the grass cost spreads across everyone. Show how individually rational choices aggregate into collective depletion, why blame alone misses the incentive structure, and how property rights, quotas, monitoring, norms, and repeated-game enforcement can change outcomes.

## 4. The Tragedy of the Commons

Template: `VideoAvatarTemplate`

Style: host in clean studio; slides in `tilt_shift` miniature-pasture realism plus hard-edged payoff diagrams.

Scene scripts:
1. Host: "The tragedy of the commons starts with one herder making one reasonable decision."
2. Slide: "One extra cow gives him milk, meat, status, and cash."
3. Slide: "The grass cost spreads across every family using the pasture."
4. Slide: "So the private reward is visible. The shared damage is blurry."
5. Slide: "Now every herder faces the same arithmetic."
6. Slide: "Refuse the extra cow, and your neighbor gets richer while you protect his grass."
7. Slide: "Add the cow, and you get paid before the field dies."
8. Host: "That is the ugly part. The bad choice can be individually correct."
9. Slide: "Fisheries work the same way when every boat races the next boat."
10. Slide: "Groundwater works the same way when every pump lowers the same aquifer."
11. Slide: "Traffic works the same way when every driver grabs the same shortcut."
12. Slide: "The villain is the payoff table."
13. Slide: "The defender is a boundary people can actually enforce."
14. Slide: "A commons survives when users can see, punish, and limit overuse."
15. Host: "Moral lectures do less than rules that change the math."
16. Slide: "Sometimes the rule is a quota."
17. Slide: "Sometimes it is access rights."
18. Slide: "Sometimes it is local monitoring by the people using the resource."
19. Slide: "The goal is not perfect kindness."
20. Slide: "The goal is making destruction stop paying."
21. Slide: "A commons dies when private gain outruns shared memory."
