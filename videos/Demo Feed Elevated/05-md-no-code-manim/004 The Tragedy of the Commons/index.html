The tragedy of
the commons starts
with one herder
making one reasonable
decision.
One extra cow
gives him milk,
meat, status, and
cash.
The grass cost
spreads across every
family using the
pasture.
So the private
reward is visible
while the shared
damage is blurry.
Now every herder
faces the same
arithmetic.
Refuse the extra
cow, and your
neighbor gets richer
while you protect
his grass.
Add the cow,
and you get
paid before the
field dies.
That is the
ugly part.
The bad choice
can be individually
correct.
Fisheries work the
same way when
every boat races
the next boat.
Groundwater works the
same way when
every pump lowers
the same aquifer.
Traffic works the
same way when
every driver grabs
the same shortcut.
The villain is
the payoff table.
The defender is
a boundary people
can actually enforce.
A commons survives
when users can
see, punish, and
limit overuse.
Moral lectures do
less than rules
that change the
math.
Sometimes the rule
is a quota.
Sometimes it is
access rights.
Sometimes it is
local monitoring by
the people using
the resource.
The goal is
not perfect kindness.
The goal is
making destruction stop
paying.
A commons dies
when private gain
outruns shared memory.
