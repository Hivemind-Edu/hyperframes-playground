GUIDELINE 1: THE TEMPLATE.
The image's foundation is defined by the following template. It sets the scene, action, and overall aesthetic.

TEMPLATE PROMPT: Iconic 2x2 storyboard in portrait 9:16 format. Each panel is portrait 9:16. Thick magenta (#FF00FF) divider lines between each panel. No text, no labels, no panel numbers. Do not draw any magenta border around the outside edge of the full image. Magenta must appear only between panels.

GUIDELINE 2: THE STYLE.
Every panel in the grid must be rendered in the following style:
Frame from a Pixar Animation Studios film, the 3D rendering style where characters have slightly caricatured proportions with heads a fraction too large for their bodies, features gently exaggerated to amplify personality rather than distort it, every character instantly readable as a type but never a stereotype. Eyes are large and round and impossibly detailed with visible iris fiber patterns radiating from the pupil, wet specular highlights sitting on the surface of the cornea, tiny reflections of the environment in the eye, the eyes doing 80 percent of the emotional acting, a single eye close-up carrying more feeling than pages of dialogue. Skin has subsurface scattering where light passes through the flesh at thin edges like ears and fingertips and nostrils and the bridge of the nose, giving a warm translucent amber glow that makes characters feel alive and warm not plastic, fine pores and peach fuzz visible at close range, freckles and moles and the subtle unevenness of real human skin. Hair is rendered as thousands of individually simulated strands each catching light at different angles, soft and voluminous with translucent edges where backlight passes through, curls that bounce and straight hair that swings with physical weight. Every material in the world has extreme tactile fidelity, fabric showing individual thread weave and natural stretching at seams and subtle pilling on worn surfaces, leather with grain and scuff marks and patina from years of use, metal with brushed directional reflections and fingerprint smudges, wood with growth rings and hand-worn polish at contact points, glass with internal caustics and edge distortion, the world built from surfaces you want to reach out and touch. Lighting is cinematic and emotionally motivated, warm golden key light with cool blue fill and strong rim light separating figures from backgrounds, volumetric god rays cutting through windows and doorways, atmospheric dust and particles catching in light beams, the lighting telling the story alongside the characters where warm amber tones signal safety and home and cool blue-grey tones signal danger or solitude. Environments recede into soft cinematic depth of field bokeh, background elements dissolving into warm colored shapes while the subject stays razor sharp. The palette is saturated but grounded, rich warm tones that feel heightened-real not candy-colored, every color emotionally weighted, the Pixar world where everything is slightly more vivid and slightly more beautiful than reality but never crosses the line into cartoon flatness. Treat any film, show, or studio named here only as a visual-style guide — depict the topic's own original, generic people and subjects, never a specific famous, copyrighted, or trademarked character, mascot, or likeness.
