## 8. The Fermi Paradox

Template: `VideoAvatarTemplate`
Role/subrole: `breakdown / system_explainer`
Scene count: 21
Style: host in clean studio; slides use astronomy-only visuals: planets, exoplanet systems, stars, Milky Way, telescopes/radio observatories, probes, galaxy maps, and empty signal monitors. No cells, microbes, microscopic imagery, biology closeups, rubber alien imagery, or text/labels inside generated images.

Scene scripts:
1. Host: "The Fermi Paradox starts with one mismatch: the galaxy looks fertile, but the sky stays quiet."
2. Slide: "First input: the Milky Way contains hundreds of billions of stars."
3. Slide: "Second input: many of those stars are older than the Sun by billions of years."
4. Slide: "Third input: planets are common, including rocky worlds in stable orbits."
5. Slide: "Fourth input: probes and signals can cross interstellar distances without magic."
6. Slide: "So even slow expansion could spread through the galaxy on timescales shorter than the galaxy's age."
7. Slide: "The expectation is not one visitor at Earth; it is some detectable trace somewhere."
8. Host: "The paradox is the gap between that expectation and what our instruments actually report."
9. Slide: "Radio observatories listen, but the monitors remain empty."
10. Slide: "Telescope surveys map exoplanets, but not clear engineered patterns."
11. Slide: "Probe paths cross the solar system, but no confirmed outside artifact appears."
12. Slide: "Galaxy maps show opportunity; observations show no confirmed civilization signal."
13. Slide: "One explanation moves the bottleneck early: worlds may be common while life is rare."
14. Slide: "Another moves it later: intelligence, technology, or long survival may be rare."
15. Host: "This is why people call it a filter: somewhere, most paths stop before they become visible."
16. Slide: "The filter could be behind us, making Earth unusually lucky."
17. Slide: "The filter could be ahead, making technological survival the dangerous step."
18. Slide: "Or the signal model could be wrong: civilizations may be distant, brief, quiet, or hard to detect."
19. Slide: "They may explore slowly, avoid broadcasting, or leave traces our current surveys miss."
20. Slide: "The Fermi Paradox does not prove we are alone."
21. Slide: "It says our simple assumptions predict a louder galaxy than the one we can measure."
