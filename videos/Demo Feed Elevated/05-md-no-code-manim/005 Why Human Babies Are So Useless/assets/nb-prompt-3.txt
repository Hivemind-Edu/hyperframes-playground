GUIDELINE 1: THE TEMPLATE.
The image's foundation is defined by the following template. It sets the scene, action, and overall aesthetic.

TEMPLATE PROMPT: Iconic 3x3 storyboard in 16:9 widescreen format. Thick magenta (#FF00FF) divider lines between each panel. No text, no labels, no panel numbers. Do not draw any magenta border around the outside edge of the full image. Magenta must appear only between panels.

GUIDELINE 2: THE STYLE.
Every panel in the grid must be rendered in the following style:
Rounded stick figure illustration on pure white background, the modern simplified human form seen in Kurzgesagt character sheets and Headspace app illustrations. Characters are soft rounded blob-like bodies with smooth continuous black outlines of uniform thickness, NOT traditional line-art stick figures. Perfect circle heads with two dot eyes and a curved mouth line, smooth capsule-shaped white bodies, rounded tube arms and legs, the entire figure defined by a single clean black outline filled with flat white or very light warm grey. No shading, no gradients, no detail beyond the essential form. Emotional expression carried entirely through minimal face and body posture: tilted head for curiosity, raised arms for excitement, hunched shoulders for sadness. Props use the same clean outline style with ONE flat saturated accent color for emphasis — a red heart, an orange book, a blue phone. Pure white background with absolutely nothing else, no ground line, no shadow, no environment. One figure or object per image, never a scene. Radical simplicity creates maximum emotional clarity and educational authority.

GUIDELINE 3: THE SPECIFICS.
Panel 1: A montage of human growth stages: a crawling 1-year-old, a playing 5-year-old, and a 10-year-old learning to hunt. Throughout the montage, an adult is always present in the frame, providing food or guidance. A calendar graphic in the corner fast-forwards through years of constant care.
Panel 2: A split-screen showing two children in vastly different environments. On the left, a child in an Arctic setting learns to recognize types of snow and track seals. On the right, a child in a tropical jungle learns to identify edible plants and mimic bird calls. Their brains have specialized for their specific survival needs.
Panel 3: A series of animal vignettes. A polar bear cub with thick fur; a desert lizard with scales; a mountain goat with specialized hooves. Each is born with the 'hardware' needed for its specific niche. They are efficient but inflexible; they cannot survive outside their biological programming.
Panel 4: The same animal vignettes, camera panning to a human child standing in the center. The child is wearing simple furs in the cold and light wraps in the heat. The child’s brain is shown as a glowing, blank-slate interface capable of downloading the specific 'software' of any culture or climate it is born into.
Panel 5: A side-by-side growth chart comparing a human to a chimpanzee. The chimpanzee reaches reproductive maturity in 8-10 years. The human timeline stretches out to 15-18 years. The 'extra' time is labeled 'Cultural Acquisition Period,' showing a huge window for learning complex behaviors.
Panel 6: A close-up of an adult's hands teaching a child how to knap flint into a tool. The child's eyes are focused, and their hands mimic the adult's movements. Inside the child's brain, neural pathways for fine motor control and tool-making logic are solidifying. This is the transmission of human knowledge.
Panel 7: A portrait of a serene human newborn. The background is a blurred collage of human achievements: cave paintings, stone tools, and fire. The infant's apparent 'uselessness' is reframed as a state of maximum potential, a biological pause that allows for the acquisition of complex culture.
Panel 8: The same portrait, camera pulled back to show the baby being passed from one pair of hands to another. The skin-to-skin contact between generations is the focus. The baby's fragility is the catalyst that creates the social bonds and collective intelligence of the human species.
Panel 9: A final scene showing a modern classroom where a teacher shows a young student a tablet. The student's brain, still plastic and receptive, absorbs the information. The image dissolves into a prehistoric elder telling a story to a child by a fire. The fundamental biological trade-off remains the same across time.

GUIDELINE 4: THE IMAGE.
Generate the 3x3 grid with thick solid bright magenta lines between panels only, all 9 panels equally sized, no gaps between panels, and absolutely no magenta frame around the outside edge of the whole image. Every panel must be rendered in the style defined in Guideline 2.
