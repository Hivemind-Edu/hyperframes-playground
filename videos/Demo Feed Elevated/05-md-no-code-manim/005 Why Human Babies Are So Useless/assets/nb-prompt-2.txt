GUIDELINE 1: THE TEMPLATE.
The image's foundation is defined by the following template. It sets the scene, action, and overall aesthetic.

TEMPLATE PROMPT: Iconic 3x3 storyboard in 16:9 widescreen format. Thick magenta (#FF00FF) divider lines between each panel. No text, no labels, no panel numbers. Do not draw any magenta border around the outside edge of the full image. Magenta must appear only between panels.

GUIDELINE 2: THE STYLE.
Every panel in the grid must be rendered in the following style:
Rounded stick figure illustration on pure white background, the modern simplified human form seen in Kurzgesagt character sheets and Headspace app illustrations. Characters are soft rounded blob-like bodies with smooth continuous black outlines of uniform thickness, NOT traditional line-art stick figures. Perfect circle heads with two dot eyes and a curved mouth line, smooth capsule-shaped white bodies, rounded tube arms and legs, the entire figure defined by a single clean black outline filled with flat white or very light warm grey. No shading, no gradients, no detail beyond the essential form. Emotional expression carried entirely through minimal face and body posture: tilted head for curiosity, raised arms for excitement, hunched shoulders for sadness. Props use the same clean outline style with ONE flat saturated accent color for emphasis — a red heart, an orange book, a blue phone. Pure white background with absolutely nothing else, no ground line, no shadow, no environment. One figure or object per image, never a scene. Radical simplicity creates maximum emotional clarity and educational authority.

GUIDELINE 3: THE SPECIFICS.
Panel 1: A detailed medical illustration of a newborn's skeletal structure. The fontanelles — soft spots on the head — are visible where skull plates have not yet fused. The cervical vertebrae are thin and lack the muscle attachments needed for support. The hands are shown in a reflexive grasp, unable to support weight.
Panel 2: A wide shot of a Pleistocene-era landscape. A mother sits near a cave entrance, cradling her infant. Predators move in the distance. The infant is a heavy, noisy, and vulnerable burden that slows the mother down and requires constant physical contact for temperature regulation and protection.
Panel 3: The same cave scene, camera zoomed in on the baby's face. The baby's eyes are locked onto the mother's eyes. A glow emanates from the baby's brain, with synaptic connections forming rapidly in response to the mother's facial expressions and soft vocalizations. The environment is now the primary architect of the brain.
Panel 4: A microscopic view of neural dendrites in a newborn's brain. As a background sound of a heartbeat and soft talking plays, new connections — synapses — visibly spark and branch out across the frame. The network is growing exponentially, fueled by the sensory input of the outside world.
Panel 5: An ancient campsite at dusk. Several adults are gathered around a fire. One woman holds the baby, but another person is preparing food for her, and a man is sharpening a tool nearby. The presence of the helpless infant forces a division of labor and high levels of cooperation among the adults.
Panel 6: The same campsite scene, camera pulls back to show the harsh environment: rugged terrain, dark woods, and the physical toll on the mother. She looks exhausted, nursing the child while trying to tend a fire. The sheer caloric and safety requirements of raising the child alone are visually depicted as impossible.
Panel 7: A multi-generational scene in a prehistoric dwelling. An older woman (grandmother) watches the toddler, while a younger male (sibling) brings water. The mother is able to sleep nearby. The infant is the center of a web of 'alloparents' who all contribute to its survival and development.
Panel 8: A diagrammatic representation of a social network. At the center is a bright icon of an infant. Lines connect it to icons representing 'Mother,' 'Father,' 'Grandparent,' and 'Kin.' Arrows of 'Energy/Food' and 'Protection' flow from the outer icons toward the center. This is the human survival strategy.
Panel 9: The same social network diagram, camera rotating to a 3D perspective. The 'social engine' label appears. The infant's presence is shown to strengthen the bonds between the adults, as they must communicate and coordinate more effectively to ensure the child survives. The baby is the glue of the tribe.
