Human babies arrive
with the coordination
of a badly
packed nervous system.
A foal stands
in hours.
A human newborn
cannot lift its
head.
Same mammal class.
Completely different launch
condition.
The old explanation
points to the
pelvis.
Upright walking reshapes
the birth canal
into a narrow,
twisted route.
At the same
time, the human
brain expands into
an expensive organ.
A larger skull
and a narrower
exit collide in
one body.
Birth moves earlier
because waiting longer
gets dangerous.
The baby leaves
the womb before
the motor system
is ready.
The skull is
flexible.
The neck is
weak.
The hands cannot
help.
Helplessness looks like
a defect from
the outside.
From the inside,
it moves development
into the social
world.
The brain keeps
wiring after birth,
under faces, voices,
touch, and language.
That long unfinished
period changes the
entire group.
One adult cannot
easily feed, protect,
carry, and teach
for years alone.
Human childhood pulls
in fathers, siblings,
grandparents, and unrelated
helpers.
Biologists call this
cooperative breeding.
The baby becomes
a social engine
by being impossible
to raise solo.
The cost is
years of dependence.
The payoff is
a brain shaped
by its local
world.
Other animals arrive
ready for one
environment.
Humans arrive ready
to be trained
by almost any
environment.
That is why
infancy lasts so
long.
Culture gets time
to enter the
nervous system.
The useless baby
is not a
failed adult.
It is an
unfinished brain wrapped
in a body
that forces cooperation.
Human helplessness is
the price of
human teachability.
