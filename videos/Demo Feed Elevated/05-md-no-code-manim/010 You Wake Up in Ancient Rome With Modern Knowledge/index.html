What if a
modern water engineer
woke up in
ancient Rome?
Day 1: You
wake beside the
Forum.
Phone dead.
Shoes wrong.
Latin everywhere.
Then you smell
the part the
marble hides.
Day 2: The
water official inspects
an aqueduct.
He measures by
rank, habit, and
favors.
Illegal pipes hide
under respectable stone.
The rich get
fountains.
The upper floors
get thirst.
Day 3: You
draw the system
in wax.
Same water.
Calibrated outlets.
Public measurements.
A builder asks
who gave you
authority.
You say water
theft hides inside
bad measurements.
The workers understand
before the officials
do.
By sunset, the
quiet pipes are
being checked.
Day 4: The
contractors hear your
name.
Guards arrive before
breakfast.
They leave the
diagram untouched.
They call you
unauthorized.
Day 5: A
Greek surveyor sits
beside you.
He says Rome
loves numbers until
numbers cost money.
You tell him
the gauges stay
public.
He smiles like
he has waited
years.
Day 6: A
magistrate demands the
tablets.
He says the
old system keeps
order.
The workers keep
copying your numbers.
Day 7: They
come for you
at dawn.
The street is
already full.
Rome needs no
future machine.
It needs honest
measurements.
