CONTENT_BRIEF:
Correct the misconception that quantum entanglement is telepathy or faster-than-light messaging. The novice should confidently misunderstand it as instant communication. The teacher should explain correlations, measurement, Bell tests, no-signalling, why outcomes are linked but not controllable messages, and why relativity is not violated. Make it sharp, funny, and technically honest.

## 6. Quantum Entanglement Is Not Telepathy

Template: `GameplayQATemplate`
Role/subrole: `misconception`
Dialogue beat count: about 15
Background set: `subwaysurfers-city`
Character style: adult cartoon cutouts on magenta. Teacher: exhausted quantum physicist. Novice: overconfident sci-fi fan.

Dialogue:
1. Novice: "So quantum entanglement means two particles are linked like a magic walkie-talkie."
2. Teacher: "Quantum entanglement means correlated measurement outcomes, not faster-than-light messaging."
3. Novice: "But the other particle changes instantly."
4. Teacher: "You do not choose the result. That is the part your space-telepathy fantasy keeps hiding."
5. Novice: "I cannot set my side to one?"
6. Teacher: "You can measure. You cannot command. The outcome is random on your side and random on theirs."
7. Novice: "Then why does anyone care?"
8. Teacher: "Because the two random lists match in a way classical physics cannot fake."
9. Novice: "So the universe coordinates randomness?"
10. Teacher: "Uncomfortably, yes. But coordination is not communication."
11. Novice: "How do we prove the match?"
12. Teacher: "Both labs compare notes later through boring old light-speed communication."
13. Novice: "So the quantum part makes the weird pattern."
14. Teacher: "And the classical channel tells you the pattern exists. No channel, no message."
15. Novice: "So quantum entanglement is real weirdness, but not remote control."
16. Teacher: "Spooky, yes. Telepathy, no."
