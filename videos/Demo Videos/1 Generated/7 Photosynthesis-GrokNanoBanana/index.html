How Photosynthesis Works
Plants do not eat sunlight.
They use it like a battery charger.
Inside each leaf cell,
chloroplasts catch light.
Light energy splits water
into hydrogen, electrons, and oxygen.
Oxygen is not the goal.
It is leftover waste.
Carbon dioxide slips in
through tiny pores.
The plant now has carbon,
hydrogen, and captured energy.
The Calvin cycle locks
carbon atoms into sugar.
Glucose stores energy
the plant can use later.
Stored sunlight becomes leaves,
roots, fruit, and food.
Photosynthesis turns sunlight, water,
and air into life.
