The year is
1971, and foreign
central banks are
draining America's gold
reserves.
West Germany and
France are aggressively
exchanging their massive
dollar reserves for
physical gold bars.
On August 13,
President Richard Nixon
gathers his top
economic advisers for
a highly confidential
meeting at Camp
David.
The team scrambles
to draft a
plan to protect
the United States
Treasury.
Two nights later,
on Sunday evening,
Nixon addresses the
nation on live
television.
He announces a
radical New Economic
Policy to stabilize
the economy.
His most dramatic
move is closing
the gold window,
suspending convertibility.
This action prevents
foreign central banks
from trading paper
dollars for American
gold vaults.
Nixon describes the
suspension as a
temporary emergency measure.
Instead, this unilateral
decision permanently dismantles
the post-war Bretton
Woods system.
The global economy
enters a new
era of pure
fiat currency.
